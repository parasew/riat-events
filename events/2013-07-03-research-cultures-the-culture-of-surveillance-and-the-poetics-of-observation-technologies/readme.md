## About the Exhibition

**"The Culture of Surveillance and the Poetics of Observation Technologies"** was a comprehensive exhibition exploring surveillance work, observation technologies, and the human aspects of monitoring systems. The exhibition was part of the larger "faceless" exhibition series at MuseumsQuartier Vienna.

### Exhibition Components

#### 1. **Artistic Bokeh Showroom: Documenting Surveillance**
- **Type**: Installation
- **Concept**: An abandoned surveillance room showing leftovers of an active operation
- **Details**: The work environment, tools, and personal items of a surveyor produce narratives of what might have happened, while showing small but significant flaws in the story
- **Technology**: Surveillance equipment still in working condition
- **Narrative**: The installation creates a meta-narration forcing critical observation of facts and fiction, with intentional anachronisms in technology

#### 2. **"Asymmetric Love No. 2" by Addie Wagenknecht and Stefan Hechenberger**
- **Type**: Public installation (viewable from July 2013)
- **Concept**: A baroque chandelier composed entirely of surveillance cameras
- **Artists**: Addie Wagenknecht and Stefan Hechenberger
- **Significance**: Mimics familiar comfort while revealing surveillance infrastructure

#### 3. **Performance "Anonymity" by Wagenknecht and Hechenberger**
- **Date**: July 3, 2013, 19:00 (opening night)
- **Location**: MuseumsQuartier Vienna
- **Concept**: 600 black glasses distributed to visitors to censor eyes, making them 'faceless' and anonymous
- **Documentation**: Photographed by Artistic Bokeh
- **Origin**: Originally developed in New York in 2007, adapted for FACELESS exhibitions

## Themes and Research

### Surveillance Work
The exhibition examined surveillance work through the lens of the U.S. Department of Labor's description: "stress, danger, confrontations with angry or upset individuals, physical discomfort, lethal hazards, fieldwork in high crime areas, monotony, constant alertness to threatening situations, irregular hours, and a heavy toll on private life."

### Human Aspects of Surveillance
- **Surveillance workers** as "reflexive and knowledgeable social actors" (Ball, 2005)
- **The "humanity of the surveillance worker"** as a "weakness of surveillance systems" (Maxwell, 2005)
- **Individual biographies** of surveillance workers that are rarely recorded
- **Ethical impulses** and the need for "great self-discipline to control unproductive ethical impulses to look away"

### Observation Technologies
- **Poetics of surveillance equipment**
- **Aesthetics of observation systems**
- **Technology as narrative device**

## Context

### Part of "faceless" Exhibition Series
The exhibition was presented as part of the "faceless" exhibition exploring anonymity, identity, and surveillance in contemporary society.

### Artistic Bokeh Research
The exhibition represented Artistic Bokeh's research into surveillance culture, observation technologies, and the intersection of art with social and political systems.

## Significance
This exhibition provided a critical examination of surveillance culture through artistic research, exploring both the technological systems and the human experiences of surveillance work. It combined installation art, performance, and documentation to create a multi-layered investigation of observation technologies and their social implications.
