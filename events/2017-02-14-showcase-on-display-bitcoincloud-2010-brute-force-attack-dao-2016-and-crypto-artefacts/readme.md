### Description
This showcase presented three significant crypto-artefacts from RIAT's history:

1. **Bitcoincloud (2010/2011)** - An interactive media-arts installation and reactive sculpture thematizing artistic production and alternative economics. The modular artwork consists of a "Bitcoin Mining Rig" (modified computers for calculating Bitcoin) and an LED cloud visualization. Bitcoincloud demonstrates the direct relationship between audience attention and artwork value: the more viewers watch the sculpture (tracked via motion detection), the more Bitcoin is mined, increasing the installation's literal value.

2. **Brute-Force-Attack (DAO, 2016)** - Documentation and analysis of the 2016 DAO (Decentralized Autonomous Organization) attack on Ethereum, which resulted in the loss of approximately $50 million worth of Ether. This event led to a controversial hard fork of the Ethereum blockchain and raised fundamental questions about immutability, governance, and security in decentralized systems.

3. **Crypto-Artefacts** - A collection of physical and digital objects representing the material culture of cryptography and blockchain technology, including hardware wallets, mining equipment, cryptographic tools, and documentation of cryptographic protocols.

### Bitcoincloud Project Details
Bitcoincloud was created by Damian Stewart, Max Gurresch, and Matthias Tarasiewicz (Artistic Bokeh) in 2011. The sculpture acts as an interface to thematize the discourse on the market for new media artworks. By excluding itself from the actual art market, it creates its own logic while still being feasible, directly connecting the attention of the viewers to its calculated value.

### Historical Significance
This showcase highlighted the evolution of cryptographic and blockchain-based art and research at RIAT, from early explorations of cryptocurrency as artistic medium (Bitcoincloud, 2011) to critical analysis of major blockchain events (DAO attack, 2016) and the collection of material artefacts that document this technological and cultural history.
